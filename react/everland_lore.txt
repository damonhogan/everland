Chapter 21: The Dragon Lantern Festival



Mage Damon's mind was clouded, memories of the fateful night before the disappearance faded, but now, fragment by fragment, they started to piece together. He recalled the evening when Tammis, along with her sister Saga, had invited him to the mystical Dragon Lantern Festival. As the recollection unfolded in his mind, the narrative within the festival played out vividly.



"Ladies and gentle folk, we bid you to gather around the stage for a revered tradition, an irreplaceable part of the Dragon Lantern Festival, where we recount the legend of our land, a place many call home. So gather 'round as we regale you with the tale of the timeless battle—Everland," the storyteller announced, as the music began, setting the stage for the narrative.



Long ago, in a realm where the land was young and untainted, beyond the Earth, three realms coexisted. Aurora with its snow-capped peaks, Lore with its radiant cities and rolling fields, and Mythos with its lush jungles and white cliffs. Yet, within the heart of Mythos, a malevolent force brewed, seeking to corrupt the lands and its people. Banished from Mythos, this chaotic entity traveled to beautiful Lore, where he perfected his darkness, stealing light and perverting all that stood in his path. This looming malevolent force became known as The Darkness, and it spread fear across realms. When the darkness opened a portal to Earth, a beloved King named Lowden, vowed to defend his realm against this encroaching threat. Though outnumbered, Lowden's warriors valiantly fought and stood their ground against the darkness. In Mythos, a rift divided its inhabitants—some chose to protect their realm, while others succumbed to the lure of darkness. Eventually, Mythos united to face the encroaching evil, and the warring armies clashed in an epic confrontation, which became known as the Battle for Everland.



Months of struggle passed, and the worsening morale of the fighting factions cast a shadow of doubt over the possibility of victory. However, with the surprising arrival of the Dragon Queen of Mythos, the tides turned. Renowned for her flames, she proved to be a pivotal force in aiding the armies, ultimately leading to the defeat of The Darkness. Sacrifices were made, and in the end, King Lowden was laid to rest. The armies, yearning for peace, buried their weapons beneath the fallen temple and left the land to heal, returning each year to honor the heroes of the battle.



As the festival's narrative reached its crescendo, a deep silence descended upon the attendees. The crackling torches cast elongated shadows, enveloping the clearing in an intense, almost ominous ambiance. Mage Damon, furrowing his brow, pondered the tale's implications. Yet, the sense of foreboding lingered within him, amplified by the absence of Tammis, Saga, and the rest of the townsfolk.



Meanwhile, Cripple Samuel, wide-eyed and fearful, remained the only other figure left in the eerily silent assembly. Gazing around, Damon's attempts to locate his friends and neighbors bore no fruit. A growing unease pervaded the gathering, replacing the earlier joy with a palpable sense of disquiet.



Determined to unravel the mystery shrouding the disappearance, Mage Damon approached Samuel. "Did you witness anything peculiar before our friends and neighbors vanished?" he inquired, his voice edged with concern.



Shaking his head, Samuel muttered, "N-no, Damon. One moment, they were there, and the next...they were gone. Vanished into thin air."



Contemplating the events that had transpired, Damon sensed an elusive, lingering magic, like wisps of a fading enchantment. "Stay here, Samuel," he instructed, his resolve firm. "I will seek the source of this enigma. We cannot afford to lose more of our people."



Intrigued and perturbed, he ventured into the woods, his senses attuned to every disturbance or trace of magic. The moon bestowed an eerie radiance as he advanced further into the forest, guided only by his determination to uncover the truth.



After what felt like an arduous expedition, he stumbled upon a clearing where the remnants of a faintly glowing portal shimmered. Its presence, though fading, remained undeniable, tendrils of residual energy fluttering through the air. As he studied the portal, he realized that the ancient Celtic temple, mentioned in the festival's narrative, stood before him. The very gateway that connected Lore to Mythos and, by extension, to Earth.



A flash of realization surged through him, as he contemplated the festival's tale. It seemed more than just a story—a conduit for ancient magic, manifesting a physical connection to a bygone era. The darkness, the chaos spoken of in the narrative, and the inexplicable disappearance of the townsfolk—these events appeared irrefutably intertwined.



Refusing to succumb to uncertainty, Mage Damon made a resolute decision. Taking a deep breath, he stepped through the portal, prepared to plunge into the heart of the enigma, ready to untangle the perplexing web that had ensnared their once jubilant celebration.


Chapter 22: Hope of Light

On the muddy roads to Everland, a damsel wanders, lost within the magic forest of mist, her steps drawn toward dreams that shimmer just beyond reach. In her quest, she seeks the enigmatic Damon of her Dreams, and a blue light surges ahead, a steadfast lantern in the murk, guiding her toward the invisible town of Everland.

Forces press in from every side, tugging her left and right, trying to draw her astray, yet the blue glow holds true, a beacon of hope through the tangled veil. Love burns bright through the forest of mist, and her resolve grows fiercer as the light leads her on.

Suddenly, the lion of the muddy road tears at her gown, but she lifts her sword with fearless grace, cloaking herself in the lion's fur as if worn by magic. From that wild omen, she weaves a radiant new gown, spun from moonlit tapestry and forest charm, a garment that belts her courage and seals her path.

She steps forward, heart aflame, guided by the blue light, toward the dream-drenched town and the promise of a dawn where their destinies might finally entwine.


Chapter 23: First Love, First Light

At first light the town of Everland yawned awake: shutters creaked, the baker coaxed steam from his ovens, and the cobbles still held the chill of night. Mage Damon woke with the ache of old danger in his ribs, a souvenir from the last road he had walked where shadows had teeth. He dressed slowly, the habit of a man who had learned to move with his wounds, and stepped out into the street.

His cloak pulled close, he passed familiar shops—lanterns of glass and iron, a cobbler's stall, a stall of bright glass beads that winked like captive stars. The bell over Kira's apothecary tinkled as he pushed the door and the scent of crushed lavender and citrus and a thousand dried leaves rose to meet him. Shelves lined the walls like the ribs of a safe ship; jars of powders and waters were labeled in neat, looping script. Soap slept in wicker, salves sat like little moons.

She stood behind the counter as if the shop itself had given her shape: hair the color of newly spun gold, hands dusted with dried petals, eyes bright with the kind of quick, hopeful intelligence that made the morning seem shy and then bold. Kira smiled—warm, uncomplicated—the kind of smile that made Damon's ache feel remote for a heartbeat.

"Welcome," she said, stepping forward. "Come in. I see you are in need of something. How can I help you with that?"

He almost forgot the pain entirely. He found himself answering with the old, automatic modesty of a man who chose his danger for others. "Right here," he murmured, pressing a gloved hand to his side. "A small wound from my last outing. It bites now and then."

Kira frowned just enough to show she understood. "Mr. Damon," she said—Damon, a name the town said with a sort of reverence and curiosity—"I hope you rested well. I heard there was a stranger in town who appeared to be in some sort of pain. What might I help you with today?"

She moved with the economy of someone who had learned every vessel in the room as if by touch alone. "I think I might have something," she decided, and reached for a slim bottle tucked among the shelves. The glass was tinted like sea-spray; the label was tiny, hand-lettered: balm of quick mend. "Put this on the wound four times a day, and it should close quicker than you think."

He took the bottle, fingers brushing hers. The contact was brief, charged—the smallness of the world folding itself into something that might become larger. He sat on the bench by the counter as if drawn there by gravity not entirely his own.

"Thank you," he said. "I wonder—can you help me further? I am stiff and sore. Perhaps you could—" He looked up, and the sentence hung like a ribbon between them.

Kira's smile softened into something like permission. "I have a marvelous massage table in the corner. A thirty-minute session with gently scented oils will see to the stiffness, and a good night's rest will finish the job."

Damon accepted like a man unaccustomed to such tender offers. He climbed onto the table, the board creaking under his weight, and lay flat—eyes catching the lamplight for a moment before he let them close.

Kira uncapped a bottle labeled Oil of Orchid. The scent came like a memory—sweet, clean, a hint of green—and she warmed a little of it between palms before setting to work. The first pass was for the shoulders: hands deft and confident, fingers mapping the line of muscle as though she read language there. Damon felt the knots unlace under her touch. She worked down his back, careful, thoughtful; then to the legs, then the arms, mindful of the old wound but not afraid of it. When he turned over, she gave the neck a long, considerate minute and lingered over his feet with a mixture of professional thoroughness and a tenderness that surprised her.

He was laughably ticklish on the soles. A small, involuntary sound escaped him as she grazed the arch; Kira could not help the chuckle that bubbled up in response—half delight, half embarrassment. They shared a look then, something like surprise that the world had allowed such a plain, joyful moment in its steady progression of skirmishes and markets and routine.

There was a lightness to Damon now that had not been there when he entered—the pain on his side had faded to a dull companion, more the memory of a fight than the fight itself. He breathed differently, more freely, and for the first time in a long while his magic felt unbound by urgency; it hummed instead with attention. He was a man used to power in the palm of his hand, and yet now it was not the power of his craft that filled him but the power of being under another's care.

Kira, meanwhile, felt something in her chest rearrange. She had ambitions—recipes and tinctures and the quiet study of the old arts—but she had never thought the lesson of their usage could be a conversation held against skin. She had, in whisper, been an aspiring magic-user: small charms, the soft shimmer of healing threads, the hush of a sachet that kept restless spirits at bay. Today she used none of the practiced, formal conjurings that she sometimes tidied into jars. Instead she chose the simpler alchemy of attention, of steady touch, of oil warmed between fingers and a voice that kept the room safe.

"You're a mage," she said at last, words that sounded ridiculous in her mouth because they felt like stating something obvious and also like holding fire. "You're Master Damon. Do you—do you find things in your work different from what people expect?"

He smiled, a slow thing, the kind that made eyes crinkle and made a person feel noticed. "Always," he said. "They expect spectacle and thunder, and often one finds instead the patience of quiet methods. The warlocks and the scholars—" he gestured with a hand that still remembered the street and the forest and the things that waited between them—"we do our part. But there's as much power in what you give with a cup and a careful hand as there is in any spell."

Kira's cheeks warmed at that. "Then we'll call it mutual craft," she said. "You mend what is torn by quarrels; I mend what the body gives up to weariness."

When the half hour passed, he lingered on the table a moment, letting the warmth linger in his muscles. He sat up slowly and tested his side, pressing the small smear of balm between thumb and forefinger like a private talisman.

"Will you watch me," he asked casually, "as I step back into the world? There are things I must attend—duties, threads that do not wait. But…will you be here tonight? I would like to speak of other matters. Of magic. Of the things that make a man choose to go where most men do not."

Kira felt the future—unformed, palpably bright—stir in the room like the breath of a sleeping thing. "Yes," she answered, surprising herself by the calmness of it. "I like that. Come back at dusk. I'll keep the lamp lit."

He stood. The wound did not look as angry as before; his shoulders were straighter. He bowed slightly, the old respect of men who measure themselves by oaths and habit. "Then at dusk, Kira," he said. "Thank you."

She watched him go, the bell chiming his departure, and when the door closed she felt the tiny, relentless glow of something that might, over time, become much more. The morning sun found its way through the glass and dust motes swam like tiny ships on a new sea. Kira set the bottle of Oil of Orchid back on its shelf, and for the first time that season the apothecary seemed to hold not only remedies and recipes, but a possibility.

Outside, Damon walked down the street with lighter steps. The town of Everland, busy with its small cares, did not notice the small, subtle revolution that had begun in an apothecary that morning: the quiet meeting of two different kinds of craft, the beginning of an exchange that might teach them both something necessary and strange.
